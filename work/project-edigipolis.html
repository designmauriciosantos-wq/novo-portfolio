Turning hashtags into a visual identity.

EDigiPolis is a university research project that uses AI to map political engagement across social media, tracking how hashtag activity spreads and clusters around two major regions in Portugal — Porto and Viseu. I designed the brand identity and the 7-page website that presents the research to the public, academic partners, and future participants.

Visit live site →

The research maps something invisible — political engagement doesn't leave a paper trail, it leaves a hashtag trail.

That was the starting point for the identity: if the method is about tracing signals across a network, the brand needed to look and feel like a signal being tracked, not like a typical academic microsite. Every decision — the logo, the colour palette, the way text and data are presented on the site — comes back to that one idea: this is a project about mapping connections, not just publishing a report.

// LOGO

Three dots, three cities

The research isn't confined to one place. The two regions being mapped — Porto and Viseu — are the subject of the study, but the research team's work is spread across three cities: Porto, Helsinki, and Viseu. The logo marks all three as connected nodes — a small network, rather than a single institutional crest.

// VISUAL LANGUAGE

The method became the design system

Because the research works by tracking hashtags, the "#" symbol and the idea of tagged, connected nodes became a recurring visual device across the site — not just illustrative, but structural: section labels, navigation cues, and data callouts all borrow the grammar of a hashtag feed, so the interface itself hints at the method behind the research.

// COLOUR

Purple for technology, green for signal

The palette leans on two colours doing two different jobs. Purple carries the "AI / research" side of the project — it reads as technical and a little synthetic, without tipping into generic corporate-tech blue. Green is the signal colour: a nod to radar and old radio-wave displays, chosen because the research itself is a kind of radar — sweeping social media for signals of engagement and plotting where they cluster.

// DETAIL

Project phases, numbered 01 — 04

The homepage breaks the research methodology into four numbered stages — ImagiDetect+, Snap-along Ethnography, Hackathons, and CiCli-Labs + IA-ART. Turning an academic methodology into a scannable, step-by-step system was one of the core design problems on this project.
