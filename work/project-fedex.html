Making a delivery photo mean something to everyone who sees it.

Built as a take-home assignment for a Service Designer role, working from FedEx's real Picture Proof of Delivery (PPOD) service — the photo a driver takes as evidence a package was left safely. The brief: in a 20-minute presentation, show how to make PPOD more valuable, not just for the recipient checking a photo, but for every stakeholder who touches that data afterward — business shippers, consumer recipients, and FedEx itself.

// THE BRIEF

A real assignment, with real business rules

PPOD had just rolled out across Europe, and FedEx wanted to increase eligibility and usage. It's opt-in and free: available for residential deliveries where the shipper or recipient has allowed delivery without a signature, flagged by a checkbox at shipment creation. The default in Europe is indirect signature release, and couriers can change the signature option mid-transit — which meant part of the job was designing within real operational constraints, not just an open brief.

FINDING OPPORTUNITY IN EVERY PAIN POINT

A photo that only reassures the recipient is doing a fraction of the job it could.

The same PPOD image already sits at the intersection of customer service, claims, and cost. Reframing it through the CFO's lens — fewer disputed deliveries, lower claims processing cost, better evidence at the point it's cheapest to capture — turned a single customer-facing feature into a case for value across the business, not just at the doorstep.
